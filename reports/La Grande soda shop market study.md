# Soda Blitz Needs More Cars, Not Bigger Kitchens

Soda Blitz's core problem is volume. About 55 tickets a day at a healthy ~$9 ticket is roughly a third of what a well-sited single-lane drive-thru sees, and the market around it won't grow on its own: La Grande has about 13,000 people, the county has about 26,000, and both have been flat for 15 years. That means the shop has to take customers from someone else. The good news is that the shop rates well, sits on Adams Avenue among a cluster of treat and drink rivals, and already sells something almost nobody else in town sells through a drive-thru: hot soup and assembled salads. Its weakness is that customers, Google and Yelp all file it as "a soda/dessert place," so the food stays hidden. The evidence points to a clear order. First, do the cheap fixes that make the food visible and the menu faster to order from (listings, menu board, loyalty, a delivery test), costing roughly $1,000–$5,000. Second, rename once those fixes show the name is the remaining barrier. Do it as a single clean change timed with a food relaunch; most of the cost is signage, roughly $5,000–$20,000. Third, spend on kitchen equipment only after your own item-mix and hourly sales data prove food demand. Step one is ventless equipment ($1,200–$28,000). The bigger option, converting the lobby to a kitchen ($30,000–$90,000+), is too large to pay back at today's volume. Several figures below came from search excerpts because official sites (ODOT, Census, Travel Oregon, CHD) could not be opened directly. The final section lists them and says how to check each one.

## Soda Blitz's gap is traffic, not ticket size

Start with the business itself, because it frames every option. At about **$15,000 a month (~$180,000 a year)** and a ~$9 average ticket, Soda Blitz serves roughly 20,000 orders a year, or about 55 a day. Industry sources put independent drive-thru coffee shops at **$200,000–$500,000+ a year when well sited**, and small kiosks at $75,000–$150,000 ([BizMetricsHQ](https://www.bizmetricshq.com/industries/food-beverage/coffee-shop/); [Roast Launch](https://roastlaunch.com/blog/coffee-shop-drive-thru/)). Fiiz, the closest soda-and-snack franchise, reports average store sales of **$612,000–$817,000** depending on the year of its franchise disclosure ([Franchise Flippers](https://franchiseflippers.com/buying-a-franchise/brands/fiiz-drinks/); [Fiiz](https://fiizdrinks.com/franchise/)). Dutch Bros averages about **$2.1–2.2 million per shop** ([Dutch Bros FY2025](https://investors.dutchbros.com/news-releases/news-release-details/dutch-bros-inc-reports-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2025)). A single-lane coffee drive-thru in a good location typically sees **150–250 cars a day**, and a well-run fast-food lane can handle about 16 cars an hour ([Restroworks](https://www.restroworks.com/blog/drive-thru-restaurant-statistics/); [Pembroke & Co](https://pembrokeandco.com/drive-thru-speed-of-service-revenue/)). If Soda Blitz is open about 11 hours on weekdays (Yelp lists 10 AM–9 PM, [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/soda-blitz-la-grande)), 55 orders works out to about **5 cars an hour on average**. So the window has a lot of unused capacity outside peak moments. Your hourly sales report will show where those peaks are.

The cost structure is close to industry norms on paper, but the owner's unpaid time is propping it up. "Prime cost" is food-and-drink cost plus labor, the two biggest controllable expenses. Quick-service restaurants aim for about **55–62% of sales**, with labor around **20–30%** and net profit of **6–9%** ([Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-prime-cost); [StockCount](https://stockcount.io/blog/restaurant-prime-cost); [Toast labor](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/restaurant-labor-cost-percentage); [Toast margins](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/average-restaurant-profit-margin)). Drive-thru coffee guidance puts labor lower, at **18–25%** ([Brewspace](https://www.getbrewspace.com/tools/coffee-shop-labor-cost-calculator/)). Blending your drink cost (12–30%) and food cost (~30%) at a 70/30 mix gives a product cost of roughly 17–30%. Adding 30–40% labor puts prime cost somewhere around **47–70%**, with a likely midpoint near 57%. That would look normal, except that it includes no pay for the owner. Small volume also hurts productivity. At about $500 a day, two people on shift for 10 hours produce roughly **$25 in sales per labor hour**. The general restaurant benchmark is about $45, and specialty cafés aim for $85+ ([7shifts](https://www.7shifts.com/blog/restaurant-sales-per-labor-hour-splh/)). Raising prices or cutting costs won't close that gap. More orders flowing through staff who are already being paid will.

A rough illustration shows the scale needed. It is not a forecast, and your P&L will sharpen it. Extra orders in slow hours cost mainly ingredients, because the staff are already on the clock. So each additional dollar of sales might contribute 50–70 cents. To fund a modest **$3,000/month owner draw**, Soda Blitz would need roughly **$4,500–$6,000 more in monthly sales**: about **17–22 more orders a day, or 30–40% growth**. That is the yardstick for every option below. Ask how many extra orders a day it plausibly produces, and how fast it pays back.

## A flat, lower-income town with two dependable crowds

La Grande gives Soda Blitz no growth tailwind. The city went from 13,082 people in 2010 to an estimated **12,985 in 2025**, and Union County sits near **25,900** ([Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/lagrandecityoregon/PST045224); [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/county/union-county-or/)). The valley towns within about 15 minutes add about 6,000 more: Union 2,154, Elgin 1,778, Island City 1,144, Cove 623 and Imbler 248 ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2023/05/23/la-grande-union-county-population-rising-according-to-estimate/)). That makes a realistic drive-thru trade area of about **19,000–20,000 people**. People also rarely leave the county for work: only **4.5%** of Union County workers commute out, and the average commute is about 16.5 minutes ([Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/oregon/union-county-or/see-what-average-commute-union-county-or)). Since few customers are passing through, getting regulars to come back more often matters more than catching travelers.

Money is tighter here than in the rest of Oregon. Union County's median household income is **$65,661**, against $83,011 statewide. La Grande's per-person income is **$32,892**, and the city poverty rate is **20.9%**, about double the US rate. Off-campus students inflate the city figure, so the county's **14.3%** better reflects family hardship ([Census QuickFacts Union County](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/map/unioncountyoregon/INC110219); [Census QuickFacts La Grande](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/lagrandecityoregon,unioncountyoregon/BZA210221)). Average wages are about **$52,300 in the county versus $71,300 statewide** ([Business Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/biz/Publications/ezwage.pdf)), and county unemployment was **6.5%** in March 2025 ([CareerOneStop](https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/StateAndLocal/Unemployment.aspx?soccode=312021&location=Oregon&dataview=table)). All La Grande schools qualify to give every student free meals through 2029 ([LGSD](https://www.lagrandesd.org/o/lghs/article/1223388)). In practice, a $5–7 treat is affordable here, but people feel extra charges. Your own reviews already complain about paying extra for ice and syrups ([Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/soda-blitz-la-grande); [Restaurantji](https://www.restaurantji.com/or/la-grande/soda-blitz-/)). Value bundles and loyalty rewards will land better here than premium add-ons.

Two groups give the shop steady demand. The first is young people. La Grande's median age is about **33**, unusually young for rural Oregon, and **21.6%** of residents are under 18 ([Census Reporter](http://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US4140350-la-grande-or/); [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/lagrandecityoregon,unioncountyoregon/BZA210221)). Eastern Oregon University enrolled **2,704** students in fall 2025 ([HECC](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/public-engagement/Documents/Commission/Full-Commission/2026/6-October%207,%208/7.1d%20-%202025%20University%20Evaluation%20-%20Eastern%20Oregon%20University%20(EOU)%20(ORS%20352.061).pdf)), but about **53% study fully online** ([CollegeTuitionCompare/IPEDS](https://www.collegetuitioncompare.com/edu/208646/eastern-oregon-university/enrollment/)), and only **18%** of undergraduates live on campus ([U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/best-colleges/eastern-oregon-university-3193/student-life)). So the real in-town student market is roughly 1,300 people, with about 450–500 in dorms. That base is small but growing: on-campus enrollment rose **7.5% from 2023 to 2025**, and early fall 2026 numbers "show continued growth" ([EOU](https://www.eou.edu/news/eou-sees-more-students-finishing-degrees-plans-for-more-success/)). Students are in town from late September to mid-June. Fall 2026 classes began **September 28** ([EOU catalog](https://catalog.eou.edu/content.php?catoid=8&navoid=484)). That makes summer a student trough. EOU athletics just won the **2025–26 conference all-sports championship** ([EOU Athletics](https://eousports.com/news/2026/5/15/general-eastern-oregon-claims-2025-26-ccc-all-sports-championship.aspx)), which makes team and booster tie-ins worth pursuing.

The second group is shift workers. Grande Ronde Hospital has **more than 800 staff** and is the county's largest private employer ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2025/03/26/grande-ronde-hospital-employs-800-personnel-plays-vital-role-in-economy/)). It recently added five operating rooms and won a **$2.2 million grant** for a workforce center expected to bring 17,000 more patient visits a year ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2025/09/18/just-in-grande-ronde-hospital-awarded-2-2-million-grant/)). The rest of the base is EOU, the school district, county, city, state and Forest Service offices, Union Pacific, the Woodgrain sawmill and the Kronospan particleboard plant (about 124 staff) ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2024/06/18/island-city-particleboard-facility-sees-change-in-ownership/); [UCEDC](https://ucedc.org/about/economy/business-industry/)). The county has about **10,185 jobs** in total ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/regions/west/news-release/countyemploymentandwages_oregon.htm)). Hospital staff and plant crews take predictable breaks and order for groups. They are the most obvious target for a "lunch plus drink" pitch and for office or department group orders. EOU did cut its budget by **$4.8 million** in FY2025 ([Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/eastern-oregon-university-budget-deficit-staff-cuts/716516/)), so public-sector payrolls are not growing.

The LDS culture that powers dirty soda in Utah is thin here. Union County has a historic LDS stake and several wards ([FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Wards_and_Branches_of_The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_Union_Stake,_Oregon); [Church locator](https://local.churchofjesuschrist.org/en/units/us/or/la-grande-1st-ward)). Statewide, though, Oregon is only about **3.6% LDS** ([ARDA](https://www.thearda.com/us-religion/maps/us-state-maps?color=orange&m1=2_1_100_2020)), against roughly 60%+ in Utah. A sourced county figure was not available. The researchers' rough estimate was 4–8%, which is not a confirmed number. That supports the owner's instinct: in La Grande, "soda shop" reads as a youth treat or a sugar habit, not a cultural staple. Demand has to come from mainstream drive-thru and energy-drink habits.

### Traffic and tourism help the Island Avenue strip more than Adams Avenue

The exact daily traffic counts (ODOT calls them AADT, the average number of vehicles per day) for Adams Avenue, Island Avenue and I-84 could not be retrieved, so no traffic figures appear here. The rest of the evidence still sketches the pattern. The chain cluster (McDonald's, Wendy's, DQ, Subway, Arby's, Starbucks and nearby Dutch Bros) sits on **Island Avenue near I-84 Exit 261**, which is also the route to Wallowa County ([Wendy's](https://locations.wendys.com/united-states/or/la-grande/2304-island-ave); [Dutch Bros](https://locations.dutchbros.com/dutch-bros-coffee-06f30045919f); [Wikipedia: OR-82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Route_82)). That road carries the summer tourist traffic to Wallowa Lake, where the state park "often sells out in July" ([Oregon State Parks](https://stateparks.oregon.gov/index.cfm?do=park.profile&parkId=20)). Eastern Oregon's visitor spending grew fastest of any Oregon region in 2025, up **5.4% to $549.5 million** ([Hermiston Herald](https://hermistonherald.com/2026/05/07/new-report-shows-economic-impact-of-tourism-in-state-region/)). I-84 through La Grande is heavily trucked: about **43% trucks** in a 2013 report ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2013/02/22/taming-i-84/)). It also closes several times each winter, and when it does, stranded trucks fill La Grande's truck stops and motels can sell out ([TripCheck](https://tripcheck.com/maps/R5/I84_WB_MP374_216_cars_only.htm); [KPTV](https://www.kptv.com/2022/11/28/winter-weather-conditions-close-i-84-between-pendleton-la-grande/)). Soda Blitz on Adams (US-30, the downtown main street) catches less of this pass-through flow than an Island Avenue site would. That reinforces the strategy: the shop's growth has to come from locals coming more often, not from travelers.

Event peaks are worth planning around. The Union County Fair draws about **14,000–18,500 people** over four days in late July and early August ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2022/08/10/union-county-fair-draws-more-than-18000-over-four-day-event/)). The Eastern Oregon Livestock Show runs in June in Union ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2026/05/21/stock-show-is-a-june-tradition-in-union/)). The Eastern Oregon Film Festival runs in mid-October ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2026/09/26/eastern-oregon-film-festival-returns-oct-14/)). Fall hunting season also brings visitors. Fair and stock-show weeks help fill the summer gap that students leave.

## Nells-N-Out, not Dutch Bros, is the rival two doors down

The owner's list of competitors (DQ, McDonald's, Wendy's, Taco Bell, Taco Time, Dutch Bros, Antlers) misses the closest threat. **Nells-N-Out at 1704 Adams Avenue** has been a family burger drive-in since 1955. It sells burgers, chicken, hot dogs, salads, ice cream, "famous cream and mixed soda drinks," and now energy drinks, through **two drive-thru windows** with cars lining up ([Go! Eastern Oregon](https://goeasternoregon.com/2024/12/16/eat-drink-in-eastern-oregon-nells-n-out-is-the-quintessential-la-grande-experience)). That is close to the food-plus-soda identity Soda Blitz is reaching for, sold by a 70-year local institution about a block away. The same stretch of Adams also has KFC (1706), Papa Murphy's (1703), **The Local at 1508** (a drive-thru selling coffee, milkshakes, ice cream and pastries, rated 4.7 on Yelp), and Buckskin Coffee at 2224 ([KFC](https://locations.kfc.com/or/la-grande/1706-adams-avenue); [La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2021/06/29/now-open-the-local-downtowns-newest-attraction); [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/p/Buckskin-Coffee-Co-61559011144164/)). Rebranding as "burgers and sodas" would put Soda Blitz head-on against Nells-N-Out. Going after soups, salads and lighter lunches avoids that fight.

Soda Blitz still looks like **the only dedicated dirty-soda shop in town**. Searches found no Swig, Sodalicious, Fiiz or similar in La Grande. One 2025 Facebook page named "but make it dirty" surfaced, but its location and status are unconfirmed ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/p/but-make-it-dirty-61576145905199/)). The bigger threat is the national chains copying the drink. Taco Bell opened 31 drink-focused cafés in 2025 and targets **$5 billion in beverage sales by 2030** ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/taco-bell-s-live-m-s-caf-exceeded-growth-expectations-in-2025); [OC Business Journal](https://www.ocbj.com/food/restaurants/taco-bell-adds-cold-brews-in-quest-for-5b-in-beverage-sales/)). McDonald's tested dirty sodas in 500+ stores and added crafted drinks, including a dirty Dr Pepper, nationally ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/24/mcdonalds-dirty-sodas-test-september); [NXS](https://www.nxsmediawire.com/news/mcdonalds-adding-6-crafted-drinks-to-menu-after-closing-cosmcs-locations/)). Customizable Rebel energy drinks reportedly make up about **25% of Dutch Bros' sales** ([Techleap/WSJ](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/dutch-bros-customisable-energy-drinks-drive-25-of-1-6b-sales)). Dutch Bros is also rolling out hot food nationwide in 2026 ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/dutch-bros-hot-breakfast-national-competition/804911/)). Swig is growing fast, toward about **200 stores by the end of 2026**, and its investor says **stores outside Utah outperform Utah stores by 40–50%** ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/retail/swigs-dirty-soda-boom-grows-beyond-utah-investor-touts-starbucksification-soft-drinks)). It is now "moving in" on the small shops it inspired, including in Idaho Falls ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2026/08/06/swigs-move-become-starbucks-dirty/)). Nothing suggests Swig is coming to a town of 13,000 soon. Still, dirty soda alone will be less of a standout every year.

Antlers shows what wins locally. It opens at **5 AM** with up to five cars waiting, runs two stands on commuter routes, rotates **monthly flavors**, sells energy drinks, and takes app orders through Joe Coffee, where it is rated 4.7 from 213 reviews ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/?p=217385); [Joe Coffee](https://joe.coffee/locations/or/la-grande/antlers-espresso-la-grande/)). Its weak spot is long lines ([Joe Coffee](https://joe.coffee/locations/or/la-grande/antlers-espresso-la-grande/)). Soda Blitz, opening around 10 AM, does not compete for the morning commute. Chains cover late night: Wendy's is open until midnight and Arby's until 11 PM ([Wendy's](https://locations.wendys.com/united-states/or/la-grande/2304-island-ave); [Arby's](https://www.arbys.com/locations/us/or/la-grande/2602-island-ave/store-9102/)). That leaves lunch and the **after-school and afternoon (about 2–5 PM) drink run** as the natural slots for Soda Blitz. Starbucks launched Energy Refreshers to win exactly that afternoon business ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2988425/sbux-bets-on-refreshers-to-boost-afternoon-traffic:-will-it-pay-off)).

The best gap in the market is food Soda Blitz already makes. Outside the chains, the only drive-thru salads found were at Nells-N-Out, and **no other drive-thru soup turned up anywhere in town** ([Go! Eastern Oregon](https://goeasternoregon.com/2024/12/16/eat-drink-in-eastern-oregon-nells-n-out-is-the-quintessential-la-grande-experience)). A long-time downtown lunch spot at 1009 Adams closed in December 2023 with no buyer ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2023/12/19/long-time-downtown-lunch-spot-to-close-doors-dec-22/)). A new downtown food-truck pod opened in October 2026 ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2026/10/02/the-train-station-brings-food-truck-pod-new-bar-to-downtown-la-grande/)), so lunch competition downtown just got a little stiffer.

Online, the shop is well liked but mislabeled. Soda Blitz holds about **4.6 stars on Google (roughly 48–65 ratings)** and **5.0 on Yelp (6 reviews)**. Reviewers praise drink variety, friendly staff and speed ([Restaurant Guru](https://restaurantguru.com/Soda-Blitz-La-Grande); [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/biz/soda-blitz-la-grande)). But Yelp files it under **Desserts, Ice Cream & Frozen Yogurt, and Beverage Store**. Other directories call it a "soft drinks shop" or "juice bar," and the website describes it as "mocktail sodas, ice cream, and baked treats" ([Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamberofcommerce.com/business-directory/oregon/la-grande/soft-drinks-shop/2027869387-soda-blitz); [Wheree](https://soda-blitz.wheree.com/); [sodablitz.com](https://www.sodablitz.com/)). No review found mentions the soups, hot dogs or salads. "I didn't know you had food" is what every listing currently tells customers.

## The name hides the food, but renaming is the last step, not the first

The rebrand cases that worked share one feature: **the new name caught up with something customers were already doing**. Boston Chicken became Boston Market in 1995 because its menu had grown past chicken to turkey, ham and meatloaf ([The Daily Meal](https://www.thedailymeal.com/1028031/what-boston-market-was-first-called); [Supermarket News](https://www.supermarketnews.com/archive/boston-chicken-changes-name)). Dunkin' Donuts dropped "Donuts" in 2019, when drinks were already well over half of sales ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15173-coffee-top-draw-for-donut-shop-customers)). The next quarter it posted its best sales gains in four years, helped by a 30% jump in espresso drinks ([1851 Franchise](https://1851franchise.com:443/the-dunkin-recast-into-beverage-business-pays-off-brand-banks-highest-sales-in-four-years-2709865)). IHOP's playful temporary "IHOb" stunt reportedly lifted burger sales two- to fourfold. Sources disagree on the exact figure, and one strategist called it "more of a brand stunt than a brand equity-building campaign" ([FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/did-ihops-ihob-campaign-drive-sales-results-are/); [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com:443/ihop-burger-lie-ihob-sales-doubled-1830254502)). The cautionary case is a coffee roaster that tacked on a food word: Barnie's Coffee & Tea became "Barnie's CoffeeKitchen," then switched back because the name "never really resonated" ([Scott Joseph Orlando](https://scottjosephorlando.com/?p=3623)). No well-documented case was found of a small drink drive-thru renaming after adding food, so these are lessons drawn by analogy, not a proven recipe.

For Soda Blitz, the lesson is to rename only once the food is genuinely part of the habit, and to make it one decisive change rather than a half-step like "Soda Blitz Kitchen." Food is already **30% of sales and growing**. That is a real shift, comparable to Boston Chicken's, but the "didn't know you had food" comments show customers haven't caught up yet. The cheapest test is to fix listings, the menu board and messaging first (Option 1 below). If food sales jump, the name was less of a barrier than it seemed. If customers still assume "soda only," the name is the problem, and you can rename knowing why.

Google is the main technical risk. Google keeps reviews only for a **"minor" name change**, meaning the proper nouns and the services in the name stay the same and the business category doesn't change. Otherwise "it's considered a new business" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3098204?hl=en); [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/business-name-change-impact-on-reviews.52432/latest)). Dropping "Soda" would probably not count as minor. Yelp decides case by case, allows only three categories, and may require going through support to make changes ([Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/yelp-8-mo-listing.62625/post-199347); [Yext](https://help.yext.com/hc/en-us/articles/52850157111707-Yelp-Integration-Overview)). Here is where Soda Blitz's small size helps. A chain with thousands of reviews has a lot to lose. You have about **50–65 Google ratings**, which you could plausibly rebuild within a few months of a relaunch with a deliberate review push. Recent reviews also matter more to shoppers than old ones: **74% want reviews from the last three months**, 31% will only use businesses rated 4.5 or higher, and 80% favor businesses that reply to every review ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). Edit the existing Google profile rather than creating a new one. Update the website and directory listings first, and expect Google to ask you to re-verify ([SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/knowledge-articles/how-to-keep-your-reviews-after-a-business-name-change/); [Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/blog/google-my-business-name-change/)).

Costs are manageable because the owner can handle the design. Professional branding agencies charge **$15,000–$60,000+**, but a small business doing the design itself typically spends **$2,500–$7,000** on design and basic printed materials ([Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/financing-your-company-rebranding-strategy); [Oficina Económica de Galicia](https://oficinaeconomicagalicia.xunta.gal/en/faqs-ambitos/how-much-does-it-cost-to-rebrand-a-small-business/)). Physical changeovers, meaning signs, menu boards, cups and uniforms, run **$5,000–$50,000 per location**. One small restaurant paid about **$4,000 for a single exterior sign** ([Springfield Business Journal](https://sbj.net/stories/made-to-order-chapter-seventeen-wrapping-up,66993?)). For Soda Blitz, the realistic total is probably **$5,000–$20,000**, driven mostly by the pole or building sign and the drive-thru board. Cups can switch over gradually with stickers as old stock runs out. Registering an Oregon assumed business name costs **$50 every two years**, but it gives no exclusive rights to the name; only a trademark does ([FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/smallbusiness/starting-a-business/how-to-file-a-dba-in-oregon-in-5-steps.html); [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/oregon-dba-guide-how-to-register-an-assumed-business-name)). Check USPTO trademarks, the Oregon Business Registry, domain names and social handles before you fall in love with a name.

### Marketing: stay on Facebook, add loyalty and a launch moment

Facebook already performs well for you, and national data backs that up. In rural areas, only **37%** of adults use Instagram and **32%** use TikTok, compared with 55% and 42% in cities. YouTube reaches **79%** of rural adults ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/)). Instagram and TikTok are still worth using for EOU students and teens, but as a secondary channel. Local media options are two radio groups. Elkhorn Media Group runs KCMB, KWRL, KVBL and KHKF, plus a news site ([Oregon Legislature testimony](https://apps.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Downloads/PublicTestimonyDocument/244798)). Pacific Empire runs KUBQ "The Ranch" (country), KRJT (classic hits) and KLBM (talk) ([Wikipedia: KUBQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KUBQ)). There is also the La Grande Observer, now owned by Carpenter Media Group ([OPB](https://opb.org/article/2024/10/23/east-oregon-bulletin-bend-eo-media-group)). The Union County Chamber can host a ribbon-cutting ([Travel Oregon](https://traveloregon.com/plan-your-trip/oregon-welcome-centers/union-county-chamber-of-commerce/)), and EOU athletics sponsorships go through the Mountaineer Athletic Association (541-962-3835) ([EOU Foundation](https://www.eou.edu/foundation/?p=57)). No published ad rates were found for any of these, so ask each for a rate card. Square Loyalty costs **$45–$105 a month**, depending on how many visits it tracks ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/square-loyalty/)). Given how much a repeat visit is worth, it is likely the best-value tool on this list.

A relaunch can borrow from IHOb without the gimmick name. Run two to three weeks of Facebook teasers that play off "I didn't know you had food." Then hold a Chamber ribbon-cutting with radio and Observer coverage and free food samples in the drive-thru line. Pair that with a launch week of a lunch combo, a loyalty sign-up bonus, an EOU student-ID deal and a school fundraiser night. Afterward, ask customers for reviews that mention the food. This sequence is a recommendation built from the evidence above. No study measures grand-reopening returns for small restaurants.

## Ventless upgrades beat a lobby kitchen until food demand is proven

Your current setup already limits what you can cook. "Ventless" equipment cleans its own smoke and grease, so it doesn't need a roof exhaust hood. The alternative, a code-required "Type I" hood, is the big stainless canopy over a fryer or grill. Ventless is legitimate, but each piece costs a lot. Under the mechanical code, factory-built recirculating systems listed to the UL 710B standard are exempt from most hood rules. The space is still treated as a kitchen and needs general ventilation, and the exemption generally covers **electric equipment only** ([Building Code Forum, quoting IMC](https://www.thebuildingcodeforum.com/forum/goto/post?id=168067)). Oregon's 2025 mechanical code, based on the 2024 international code, became mandatory for new permit applications on **April 1, 2026** ([Oregon BCD](https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/mechanical.aspx)).

The math rules out the big paths for now. A $50,000 lobby conversion that adds $1,000 a month in profit takes **about four years to pay back** before financing costs. That profit figure is an illustrative assumption, since Dutch Bros, whose food program is far larger than its old menu, saw only about a **4-point sales lift** from adding hot food ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/dutch-bros-hot-breakfast-national-competition/804911/)). Soda Blitz is already food-heavy for a drink shop: food is 30% of your sales, against about 2% at Dutch Bros before its rollout ([Investing.com/UBS](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/ubs-reiterates-dutch-bros-stock-rating-on-food-rollout-momentum-93CH-4629729)). The next food dollars will be harder to win than the first ones were. Drink chains keep food simple and quick for a reason. Even McDonald's shut CosMc's partly because its drinks were too complex to make fast ([Fox 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/mcdonalds-closes-cosmics-drink-test)). If a food item slows the window, it can cost more in lost drink orders than it brings in.

If you do convert the lobby, expect three approvals. The first is a City of La Grande building permit (541-962-1360). The second is plan review by CHD Environmental Health, which runs Union County's food program (541-962-8800), and construction cannot start until CHD approves the plans ([CHD](https://www.chdinc.org/environmental-health); [Deschutes County packet](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/health_services/page/930/new_restaurant_plan_review_packet.pdf)). The third is fire review of any suppression system. Similar Oregon counties charge **$360–$650** for remodel plan review, and Umatilla charges about $440–$460 ([Benton](https://health.bentoncountyor.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EHF-0299N-2025-EH-Fee-Sheet.pdf); [Hood River](https://www.hoodrivercounty.gov/vertical/sites/%7B4BB5BFDA-3709-449E-9B16-B62A0A0DD6E4%7D/uploads/1-1-26_-_EH_Fee_Schedule.pdf); [Umatilla](https://co.umatilla.or.us/fileadmin/user_upload/BCC/Agendas/Item9061825.pdf)). The fee is small next to the construction. The hidden limit is **electrical capacity**: each ventless unit needs its own 208/240V circuit. Have an electrician check the panel before you price anything else.

Funding can soften the cost. Local Business Oregon small grants through NEOEDD paid **up to $4,000** for existing Union and Wallowa County businesses in 2025 ([Elkhorn Media Group](https://elkhornmediagroup.com/2025-eeip-small-grants-available-for-businesses-in-union-and-wallowa-counties)). SBA microloans go **up to $50,000** for equipment and working capital, with an average of about $13,000 ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/microloans)). The most promising lead is La Grande's **Urban Renewal grant**, which helped The Local convert a former Texaco station with a **$64,000** award in 2021 ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2021/06/29/now-open-the-local-downtowns-newest-attraction)). Its annual call for projects opened in March 2026 ([La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/2026/03/09/la-grande-opens-annual-call-for-projects-grant-application/)). Whether 1609 Adams sits inside the urban renewal district was not confirmed. Ask the city.

## Delivery and menu trends favor a lean, faster menu over a bigger one

Delivery demand is real in La Grande, but the chains dominate it. On DoorDash, McDonald's shows **6,000+ ratings** and Taco Bell **3,000+** ([DoorDash](https://www.doordash.com/en/food-delivery/la-grande-or-restaurants/fast-food/)). Uber Eats and Postmates are also active ([Uber Eats](https://www.ubereats.com/city/la-grande-or); [Postmates](https://postmates.com/city/la-grande-or)). Soda Blitz didn't appear on either platform, so the "drinks and treats delivered" slot looks open. DoorDash charges restaurants **15%, 25% or 30%** of each order on its Basic, Plus and Premier plans, and **6% on pickup** orders. Its Storefront online ordering charges only **2.9% + $0.30** in card fees, and its Drive service delivers your own online orders for a flat **$6.99–$10.99** ([DoorDash pricing](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/products/pricing); [DoorDash Drive](https://merchants.doordash.com/en-us/products/drive-on-demand); [DoorDash news](https://doordash.news/merchant/announcing-updates-to-provide-more-choice-flexibility-and-transparency-for-local-restaurants/)). Uber Eats raised its lowest tier to **20%** in March 2026 ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/uber-eats-increases-marketplace-fees/814294/)). On a $9 order, Basic costs about $1.35. If you mark delivery menu prices up 15–20% and cover your ingredients, most delivery orders that arrive in slow hours are still profitable, because your staff are already there. The 30% tiers eat most of the margin. Ice-filled drinks also travel badly. So test delivery with food leading (soup, nachos, pretzels, hot dogs) and drinks bundled with them, and keep the test short so you can drop it if it doesn't pay.

The drink trends point toward a simpler menu with a few new lines. Cold foam on menus is up nearly **1,700% since 2022**, refreshers are up **127%** in four years, and **52%** of consumers now prioritize protein, up from 38% in 2023 ([Datassential](https://datassential.com/resource/new-classics-2026-food-beverage-trends/)). Energy drinks are a **$31.2 billion** U.S. category ([Glanbia](https://www.glanbianutrition.com/en/nutri-knowledge-center/insights/energy-drink-trends-what-expect)). The headwind is GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. Households using them cut fast-food and coffee-shop spending about **8%**, with similar cuts to sweets and cookies ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29532-glp-1-users-cut-food-spending-by-53)), and an estimated **23%** of U.S. households now include a user ([Food Industry Executive](https://foodindustryexecutive.com/2026/03/what-glp-1-adoption-actually-means-for-your-sku-rationalization-decisions-in-2026/)). In practice: add a sugar-free version of each signature drink, a protein cold foam, an energy-refresher line and a smaller "lite" size. Steer food growth toward savory and protein items (soups, salads, hot dogs) rather than more sweets.

On the menu itself, 60+ syrups across 20+ drink categories is a lot to read from a car window. Research on fast-food menus found customers prefer about **six items per category** ([FoodTec Solutions](https://foodtecsolutions.com/blog/2018/10/31/do-smaller-menus-yield-more-sales/)). Broader studies show "too many choices" effects vary widely ([Columbia stat blog on Scheibehenne et al.](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2021/05/08/blast-from-the-past-2/)), so speed is the stronger argument than confusion. Swig's model is a base soda plus add-ins, named signature drinks, and build-your-own for regulars ([Wikipedia: Swig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swig_(restaurant))). Sonic, a chain known for its huge drink menu, moved to a "curated beverage lineup" ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/sonic-drive-in-to-roll-out-curated-beverage-lineup)). A practical board would have five to seven categories (for example dirty sodas, energy, lemonades/refreshers, blended, food), about six named drinks in each, and one line reading "build your own." The full flavor list would go on a QR code or a side panel. Keep the syrups; just stop making every customer read all of them. A "menu engineering" pass scores every item on how often it sells and how much it earns, then cuts the low-selling, low-margin ones ([Duck-Hub on Kasavana & Smith](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/menu-engineering-statistics)). It needs your POS item-mix report.

## What to measure and verify before committing

Your own numbers will decide this more than any market figure. When you add your files, the analysis needs five things. The **P&L** confirms the true margin and what each extra dollar of sales contributes. The **item-mix report** (units and price for every item over 90 days) drives the menu cuts and shows which foods actually sell. The **hourly sales report** shows peaks, dead hours, staffing waste and whether earlier opening (to catch energy-drink commuters) or later hours would pay. **Labor hours by day**, including your own, give the real cost of staffing. Finally, the **lease terms and electrical panel rating** determine whether any kitchen work is feasible. A simple two-week stopwatch log of drive-thru times split by drink-only versus food orders would show whether food is slowing the window.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that Soda Blitz's biggest problem isn't its kitchen, and may not be its name. It is that roughly 20,000 nearby people don't know the full reason to come back. The shop already holds the one food position nobody else in La Grande has taken, drive-thru soup and salad with a fun drink. The real competition is a block away on Adams, not out on Island Avenue. And national chains copying dirty soda make the food more valuable to the brand each year, not less. So the rebrand question is really about timing. Make the food visible and the menu faster first, measure for 90 days, then rename with evidence in hand. A shop with 65 reviews can afford a clean break in a way a chain cannot.

The decisive numbers are ones only you have: which items earn money per minute of window time, and which hours sit empty. If the item-mix and hourly reports show food raising average tickets without slowing the line, and slow afternoons responding to targeted deals, the path to an owner paycheck is about 20 more cars a day. That comes from visibility, loyalty and a tighter menu long before it needs a $50,000 kitchen.
