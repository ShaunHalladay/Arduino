## 1. Population: latest estimates, trend, projections

### Takeaway
La Grande has about 13,000 residents and Union County about 26,000, and both have been essentially flat for 15 years (within roughly ±1%). The wider three-county Northeast Oregon trade area (Union + Baker + Wallowa) holds about 50,000 people. No growth tailwind exists, so a new concept has to win share rather than ride population growth.

### Inferences
- From 2010 to 2025, La Grande went from about 13,082 to about 12,985 (−0.7%) and Union County from 25,748 to about 25,900 (+0.6%). Both are flat.
- La Grande holds about 50% of county residents. The towns within about 15 minutes (Island City, Union, Cove, Imbler, Elgin) add about 5,950, so the immediate Grande Ronde Valley population is about 19,000–20,000. The rest of the county is unincorporated rural (about 6,000).
- Regional trade area: Union 25,900 + Baker 16,746 + Wallowa 7,497 ≈ **50,100**. La Grande is the retail and medical hub for Union and Wallowa counties (Wallowa traffic comes via OR-82). Baker City is about 45 minutes away on I-84 and has its own retail.

## 2. Age distribution, households, household size, households with children

### Takeaway
La Grande is young for rural Oregon (median age about 33, pulled down by EOU), while the county overall skews older (22% aged 65+). Households are small (2.29 persons in the city), and about 1 in 5 residents is under 18.

### Cited Findings
- La Grande median age: **32.9** (±2.6), ACS 5-year (circa 2019–2023) — [Census Reporter](http://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US4140350-la-grande-or/); [Point2Homes](https://www.point2homes.com/US/Neighborhood/OR/La-Grande-Demographics.html). Data USA (2022) gives 33.1 — [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/la-grande-or-31000US29260)
- La Grande: under 18 = **21.6%**; 65+ = **18.7%**; households (2020–2024) = **5,538**; persons per household = **2.29** — [Census QuickFacts La Grande vs Union County](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/lagrandecityoregon,unioncountyoregon/BZA210221)
- Union County: under 18 = **21.4%** (Oregon 19.3%); 65+ = **22.2%** (Oregon 19.9%); households (2020–2024) = **~10,900**; persons per household = **2.35** (Oregon 2.42) — [Census QuickFacts Union County/Oregon](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/table/HSG010215/41061,41)
- Third-party summary of 2023 ACS: Union County 65+ = 21.0% — [veritasx](https://www.veritasx.us/union-county-oregon-demographics.html)

### Inferences
- About 2,800 La Grande residents are under 18 (21.6% × 12,985), and about 5,550 county residents are.
- The 18–64 band in La Grande is about 59.7% (100 − 21.6 − 18.7), or about 7,750 people, and it includes most of the roughly 500 students who live on campus (see section 5).
- The median age of about 33 in the city against an older county suggests a youth and college concentration in the city, which is the core demographic for dirty soda and energy-drink customization.

### Gaps

Pull ACS 2020–2024 5-year DP05 rows for ages 15–19, 20–24, 25–34 and 35–44, plus DP02 "households with children under 18", for place 4140350 and county 41061.

## 3. Income, poverty, SNAP, school lunch, unemployment

### Takeaway
Incomes are well below state and national levels. The county's median household income is about $65.7K (about 79% of Oregon's), La Grande's is about $56–58K, and city poverty is about 21%, roughly double the US rate (EOU students inflate this). A treat-priced beverage at $4–7 sits within budget, but price sensitivity is high, and value and loyalty pricing matter.

## 4. Race/ethnicity and cultural/religious composition (LDS)

### Takeaway
Union County is about 87% non-Hispanic white and about 6% Hispanic, far less diverse than Oregon. It has a deep historical LDS presence (the region's first stake, organized in 1901, and at least 3–4 wards today in La Grande/Island City), but its LDS share is likely in the low-to-mid single digits. That is an order of magnitude below Utah's roughly 60%+. Dirty soda demand here has to come mainly from mainstream youth and drive-thru culture (the Dutch Bros/Swig trend), not an LDS-majority base.

### Cited Findings
- Union County (ACS 2020–2024): White alone **91.6%**; White non-Hispanic **86.9%** (Oregon 71.8%); Hispanic/Latino **5.9%** (Oregon 15.5%) — [Census QuickFacts Union County/Oregon](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/table/HSG010215/41061,41)
- 2023 ACS summary: White non-Hispanic 86.37%, Hispanic 5.35%, two or more races 3.72% — [veritasx](https://www.veritasx.us/union-county-oregon-demographics.html)

### Inferences
- About 2.13M adherents ÷ about 3.27M Utah residents (2020) ≈ **65%**. This is a researcher calculation, and adherent counts include some double-counting.
- A stake typically comprises about 5–12 congregations. With at least 3 La Grande-area wards in a stake that also spans Baker and possibly Wallowa counties, Union County LDS adherence is plausibly about 4–8% (roughly 1,000–2,000 people). This is an estimate, not a sourced figure. It would be at or above the Oregon average (3.6%) but about 1/10th of Utah's share.
- Proximity to Idaho (Boise is about 3 hours away; southeast Idaho is heavily LDS) and the I-84 corridor means many residents and EOU students will already know Swig and Sodalicious-style concepts.

## 5. Top employers, industries and wages

### Takeaway
The economy rests on "eds and meds" plus public sector, rail and wood products. Grande Ronde Hospital (800+ staff) is the largest private employer. EOU, the school district, county, city, state and federal (USFS) offices, Union Pacific's rail yard, and the wood-products plants (Woodgrain sawmill, Kronospan particleboard at Island City, about 124 staff) round out the base. Shift workers at the hospital, plants and railyard are prime drive-thru customers.

## 6. Eastern Oregon University: enrollment, residence, staff, calendar, demographics, athletics

### Takeaway
EOU enrolls about 2,700–2,950 students, but about half are fully online. The in-town student body is roughly 1,300–1,400, and only about 18% of undergraduates (about 450–500) live in campus housing. The on-campus population is growing (+7.5% from 2023 to 2025) and the athletics program is strong. Students are in town from late September to mid-June (quarter system).

### Cited Findings

- Fall 2023: **2,798**, the first increase since 2019 — [Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/?p=493810)

- Fall 2025: **2,704** (−94, −3.4% vs Fall 2023). FTE rose from 1,973.1 to **2,003.5** — [HECC 2025 University Evaluation: EOU (Oct 2026)](https://www.oregon.gov/highered/public-engagement/Documents/Commission/Full-Commission/2026/6-October%207,%208/7.1d%20-%202025%20University%20Evaluation%20-%20Eastern%20Oregon%20University%20(EOU)%20(ORS%20352.061).pdf)

## 7. Commuting patterns and trade area

### Takeaway
Union County is a self-contained labor market. Only about 4.5% of workers leave the county, and commutes average about 16.5–17 minutes. Daily inflow mostly comes from valley towns (Island City, Union, Cove, Imbler, Elgin), with some from Baker and Wallowa counties. The drive-thru trade area is effectively the Grande Ronde Valley (about 19–20K), plus Wallowa County shoppers and I-84 travelers.

### Cited Findings
- 2016–2020 ACS: **4.5%** of Union County workers worked outside their county of residence. Average commute **16.5 min** (Baker 14.9; Wallowa 16.9) — [Stacker](https://stacker.com/stories/oregon/union-county-or/see-what-average-commute-union-county-or)
- Regional ED page: "Resident Commuters 10,828; Non-Resident Commuters 415; average commute 17 minutes" (year and source unclear) — [OTEC Economic Development](https://economicdevelopment.otec.coop/the-region/baker-county/p/item/631/union-county)
- OED published "Head out on the highway: Union County commuter data – 2022" (Tony Wendel, 2025). Contents were not retrievable — [State Library of Oregon](https://digitalcollections.library.oregon.gov/nodes/view/310455)
- The Pendleton corridor (about 50 min) is the most common longer commute — [Living in La Grande](https://themovegenie.com/oregon/livinginlagrande)
- Town populations: see section 1.

### Inferences
- With about 95% of workers working in-county and about 10,185 covered jobs, nearly all daytime workers in La Grande also live in the valley. Repeat-customer frequency (loyalty apps, punch cards) matters more than capturing pass-through commuters.
- I-84 runs through La Grande (about 4 hours from Boise via Ontario, about 4 hours from Portland), adding traveler traffic at the Island City/Adams Ave exits.

### Gaps
- No OnTheMap/LODES inflow-outflow counts by origin town (Island City, Union, Cove, Elgin, Imbler, Baker City, Wallowa County) were obtained. Run OnTheMap for La Grande city (work area) to get them.
- No ODOT traffic counts (AADT) for Adams Ave / Island Ave / I-84 were found. This is out of scope here, but useful for site selection.

## 8. Recent economic development news (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
2023–2026 shows modest, mixed activity. There is health care investment (GRH surgery expansion and a $2.2M workforce center), wood-products churn (Kronospan purchase, Woodgrain consolidation, Elgin layoffs), downtown and food-truck activity, the Drive-in's closure, and several mid-size housing projects plus a proposed 1,500-unit infrastructure area. The city is commissioning a new 5-year economic development plan for 2026–27.
