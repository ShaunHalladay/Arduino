## 1. State of the dirty soda segment, 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Dirty soda moved from a Utah/LDS niche to the national mainstream between 2023 and 2026. Swig grew from about 100 to about 160+ units, and its investor says stores outside Utah outperform Utah stores. Big chains copied the category: Taco Bell (Live Más Café), McDonald's (CosMc's closed, drinks folded into McDonald's), Sonic, Starbucks and Dutch Bros (energy refreshers). For a small independent, the main risk is no longer whether the concept travels. It is competition from national chains that now sell the same drinks.

### Cited Findings
**Origins / LDS culture**
- Dirty soda's roots lie in the LDS "Word of Wisdom" health code, which discourages "hot drinks" (coffee and tea). Soda is not prohibited, which created a non-coffee treat culture in Utah — [Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1541906/origin-dirty-soda/); [Chowhound](https://chowhound.com/1666125/what-is-dirty-soda-origin)
- The church clarified in 2012 that caffeine in cold beverages / non-coffee-tea drinks is not specifically prohibited (sources word this differently) — [Wikipedia: Dirty soda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_soda); [Chowhound](https://chowhound.com/1666125/what-is-dirty-soda-origin)
- Swig was founded April 2010 in St. George, UT by Nicole Robison (Tanner) and Todd Tanner, and trademarked "dirty soda" around 2013 — [Wikipedia: Swig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swig_(restaurant)); [WSU Guardian, Mar 2025](https://www.wsuguardian.com/article/2025/03/the-origins-of-dirty-soda-from-mormonism-to-mainstream)
- Reported drivers of national spread: social media, "Mormon mommy influencers," and the Utah-based reality TV show (*The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives*) — [Fronteras Desk](https://fronterasdesk.org/node/1740357); [CNN, Sept 2024](https://www.cnn.com/2024/09/28/business/dirty-soda-shops/index.html) (via summary)

### Inferences
- The category has proven itself outside Utah. Swig's out-of-state stores reportedly beat Utah stores, and national chains have adopted the drinks. A small Eastern Oregon shop is not selling an unfamiliar concept, but its uniqueness is fading.
- McDonald's verdict on CosMc's (too complex for the core operation) is a warning about menu complexity and speed. A huge chain could not make a sprawling custom-drink menu work operationally. That supports a simpler, faster menu for a small shop.
- Energy refreshers are the main battleground in 2025–2026: Dutch Bros Rebel at about 25% of sales, plus Starbucks and Dutch Bros Myst launches. For a soda shop near a Dutch Bros or Starbucks, energy drinks are both a direct threat and a must-carry item.
- Eastern Oregon sits in the Dutch Bros home region (Oregon). Swig's Idaho push (Idaho Falls, Boise area) suggests chains may move toward the Inland Northwest, though no La Grande-specific plans were found.

## 2. How specialty-drink chains are adding food

### Takeaway
Drink-led chains keep food small, simple and "snacky": cookies, pretzel bites and a few breakfast items. Food lifts sales only modestly. Dutch Bros' hot-food program gave about a 4-point comp lift where it rolled out, and roughly a quarter of its shops can't fit it. Drinks remain the profit engine.

### Cited Findings
- Dutch Bros expanded hot food from 4 shops to 300+ shops (11 states) in 2025, with full rollout targeted by end of 2026. Shops with expanded food show about a **4-percentage-point comp lift**, with one quarter of that coming from extra transactions — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/dutch-bros-hot-breakfast-national-competition/804911/); [Globe and Mail Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/Motley%20Fool/195831/dutch-bros-bros-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)
- About 25% of Dutch Bros' 2025 year-end shops can't offer hot food because of layout constraints — [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/dutch-bros-national-push-gains-steam/) (via summary)
- An analyst put food at about 2% of Dutch Bros sales (pre-full rollout) — [Investing.com / UBS](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/ubs-reiterates-dutch-bros-stock-rating-on-food-rollout-momentum-93CH-4629729) (via summary)
- Q2 2026: Dutch Bros completed the food rollout across company-operated shops and began extending it to franchise shops. AUV reached $2.19M — [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/dutch-bros-builds-growth-formula-that-keeps-delivering/) (via summary)
- Swig's food menu is limited to sugar cookies (its signature), pretzel bites and LTO treats. Third-party menu sites list pretzel bites at about $3.75–$3.99 and dips at $0.50 — [Wikipedia: Swig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swig_(restaurant)); [Swigmenus.us (third-party, low reliability)](https://swigmenus.us/pretzel-bites-swig-menu-drinks-prices-calories-2026/)
- Fiiz positions itself as a "specialty soda & snack" franchise — [Fiiz](https://fiizdrinks.com/franchise/)
- CosMc's paired drinks with snacks aimed at younger customers before it closed — [Fox 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/mcdonalds-closes-cosmics-drink-test)

### Inferences
- The subject shop's 30% food mix is far above Dutch Bros (about 2% before rollout) and probably above Swig. Food that slows the drive-thru, or needs more labor or equipment, can cost more in throughput than it earns. Lessons from the chains: keep food grab-and-go or quick-heat, attach it to drinks (cookie + soda bundles), and drop food items that slow the window.
- Food can still be a real lever in a rural market with fewer quick meal options. The shop should check food contribution margin and its effect on service time, not just food sales.

- No Dutch Bros food-specific margin data.

At about $180k/year, the shop sits at the small-kiosk end of the range: well below independent drive-thru coffee estimates ($200k–$500k+), Fiiz franchise averages ($612k–$817k) and Dutch Bros ($2.1M+). ### Inferences
- Shop math: $180k/yr ÷ $9 ticket ≈ 20,000 tickets/yr ≈ 55/day. That is roughly a third or less of the 150–250 cars/day cited for a well-sited single-lane coffee drive-thru. Ticket size is reasonable, so the gap is traffic.
- Labor at small volume: to hit a 25% labor target on $15k/month, total labor would have to be about $3,750/month. That is roughly 230 hours at an assumed ~$16/hr, before payroll taxes, or about 7.5 hours/day. (The wage is illustrative only. The current Oregon minimum wage tier for Union County was not researched here.) A shop open 10+ hours with two people on shift will almost certainly run labor well above 30% unless the owner works unpaid. Comparing owner-labor-included vs. excluded matters.
- SPLH check: at about 55 tickets/day and $500/day, two staff over 10 hours (20 labor hours) gives about $25 SPLH, far below the ~$45 general and $85 café targets. Staffing to traffic (one person in slow dayparts) or raising volume is the lever.
- Drink-heavy menus should carry a lower COGS% than food. Soda + syrup + cream drinks often cost well under 20% to make (inference, not sourced here), so shifting mix toward drinks should improve prime cost.

- No published dirty-soda-specific COGS or labor benchmarks. No Swig or Sodalicious Item 19 was found.

## 4. Menu engineering for overwhelming menus

### Takeaway
The evidence supports about 6 items per category for quick service and a curated "signature" lineup. Strict "choice overload" effects are real but variable (a meta-analysis found an average effect near zero). Speed problems from complex custom drinks are well documented, as McDonald's experience with CosMc's shows. Menu engineering means scoring every item on popularity × contribution margin, then cutting or reworking the weak ones.

### Cited Findings
- Bournemouth University research (Johns et al., 2013): fast-food customers preferred about **6 items per category**, fine dining 7–10. Some summaries cite "seven" as the golden number. The primary paper wasn't directly verified — [FoodTec Solutions](https://foodtecsolutions.com/blog/2018/10/31/do-smaller-menus-yield-more-sales/); [Terraslate](https://www.terraslate.com/blogs/news/how-many-items-menu); [Duck-Hub](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/menu-engineering-statistics)
- Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010) meta-analysis of 50 experiments / 63 conditions: the mean choice-overload effect was "virtually zero" but varied widely across studies. Overload depends on context (expertise, time pressure, complexity) — [Columbia stat blog discussion](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2021/05/08/blast-from-the-past-2/); [UNIGE archive](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:76440)
- LSE work: intermediate-size choice sets produce better cognitive and emotional outcomes than very small or very large ones — [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/113570)
- Menu engineering (Kasavana & Smith, 1982): classify items by menu mix and contribution margin (Stars, Plowhorses, Puzzles, Dogs) and redesign accordingly — [Duck-Hub](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/menu-engineering-statistics)
- McDonald's CEO said CosMc's drinks may be too complex for regular McDonald's operations, and CosMc's experimented with lanes routed by order complexity — [Fox 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/mcdonalds-closes-cosmics-drink-test)
- Sonic launched a "curated beverage lineup" (a sign that even a huge-menu chain is curating) — [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/quick-service/sonic-drive-in-to-roll-out-curated-beverage-lineup)
- Swig's structure: a base soda (Coke/Dr Pepper/etc.) plus add-ins (syrups, creams, purées), with named signature drinks alongside build-your-own, a few drink families (refreshers, energy, hot cocoa), and a very small food menu (cookies, pretzel bites, LTOs) — [Wikipedia: Swig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swig_(restaurant))

### Inferences
- Practical structure for a drive-thru board: about 5–7 categories (Dirty Sodas, Energy, Refreshers, Coffee/Cold Brew, Treats/Food), each showing roughly 6 named signature drinks, plus a single line saying "build your own: pick base + 2 flavors + cream". Put the full flavor list on a secondary board or in the app/QR code.
- Use the name-plus-number "signature" approach (as Swig, Dutch Bros and Taco Bell do). It speeds ordering and is easier to push in marketing and LTOs.
- Run a basic menu-engineering pass on the POS item mix. Cut "Dogs" (low popularity, low margin), especially food items that slow the window.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study specific to drive-thru menu boards (eye-tracking, item count) was found.
- The "70% of sales from 30% of items" claim was found only on vendor blogs without a traceable source, so it was excluded.

## 5. Consumer trends 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Trends favor cold, customizable, caffeinated drinks: energy refreshers, cold foam, protein add-ins, and less-sugar/zero-sugar options. GLP-1 adoption is measurably cutting spending on sweets, snacks and limited-service restaurants. That is a headwind for sugary drinks and cookies, and a reason to offer sugar-free and protein options.

### Cited Findings
- Datassential: cold foam menu penetration is up nearly 1,700% since 2022, with 431% growth projected through 2030. Refreshers are up 127% over four years, and 54% of operators saw refresher sales rise in the last year. ### Inferences
- Lean menu additions that match the trends: a sugar-free version of every signature, a protein cold foam or protein shake, a small "lite" size, and an energy-refresher line (the Dutch Bros/Starbucks battleground).
- GLP-1 effects hit cookies and sweets hardest. That argues against expanding the sweet-treat side of the 30% food mix, and for higher-protein or savory grab items instead.

### Gaps
- No data on GLP-1 adoption rates in rural Oregon / Union County.
- No direct data on GLP-1 users' purchases of sugary specialty drinks specifically (as opposed to sweets and snacks).
