## 1. Ventless equipment options, prices, and listing requirements

### Takeaway
Real ventless cooking is possible but expensive per unit. Each high-speed oven or ventless fryer runs about $9k-$15k new, and a ventless recirculating hood for ordinary electric equipment runs about $18k-$38k for small sizes. Electric-only operation and a UL (KNLZ / UL 710B-type) ventless listing are the common conditions.

**Code basis for "ventless"**
- Model IMC exception: factory-built commercial cooking recirculating systems listed and labeled to UL 710B and installed per IMC 304.1 are exempt from several Section 507 hood/duct requirements. The space is still treated as a kitchen and must be ventilated per Table 403.3.1.1, with each appliance counted as occupying at least 100 sq ft — [Building Code Forum (quoting IMC)](https://www.thebuildingcodeforum.com/forum/goto/post?id=168067)
- Practitioners say recirculating systems are for electric appliances only; gas appliances need outdoor venting because filters do not remove combustion products. One commenter notes general exhaust (0.7 CFM/sq ft per IMC 403.3) is still required. Forum posts are informal — [Building Code Forum](https://www.thebuildingcodeforum.com/forum/goto/post?id=168067)
- Washington State (not Oregon) added a second path: no Type I hood for an electric appliance with documented effluent of 5 mg/m3 grease or less when tested under UL 710B at 500 cfm. Oregon was not confirmed to have this amendment — [WAC 51-54A-0607](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/WACArchive/2022/htm/WAC%20%2051%20%20TITLE/WAC%20%2051%20-%2054A%20CHAPTER/WAC%20%2051%20-%2054A-0607.htm)

### Inferences
- The same mechanism (KNLZ category / UL 710B-type testing) covers both appliances with built-in catalytic recirculation (TurboChef, Merrychef) and add-on recirculating hoods (Wells WVU). The vendors' "UL KNLZ" and the code's "UL 710B" are related but not always named the same way, so get the listing file number for the building official.
- Unverified background knowledge (not confirmed by a source in this session): low-grease and steam equipment usually needs no Type I hood. This includes hot dog rollers, soup wells and kettles, rice cookers, sous vide circulators, steamers, popcorn poppers, cheese/nacho warmers, holding cabinets, toasters and, in many jurisdictions, panini/contact grills used for pre-cooked items. Open griddles, charbroilers, open fryers and ranges generally do need one. The building official decides case by case; IMC 507 hinges on whether an appliance produces grease or smoke.
- Induction hobs remove combustion but not grease. Searing or frying on induction can still trigger a hood requirement; boiling, steaming and holding usually would not (unverified; confirm with the building official).
- The cheapest expansion path is probably a second high-speed oven (or a used one, about $1-6k) plus one ventless fryer (about $11-13k), not a $20k+ ventless hood with separate griddles.

## 2. Realistic menu expansions with ventless equipment

### Takeaway
High-speed ovens can do breakfast sandwiches, flatbread and pizza, toasted wraps, grilled-style sandwiches, quesadillas and reheated proteins. A ventless fryer adds fries, tots and wings. Rice bowls, soups, baked potatoes and hot holding are generally low-risk, steam-based operations.

### Cited Findings
- The TurboChef Fire is a dedicated ventless countertop pizza oven, which makes artisan-style pizza and flatbread feasible without a hood — [TurboChef Fire spec sheet](https://turbochef.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/TurboChef_DOC-1429M-Fire-Spec-Sheet_English_Sept-2025.pdf)
- Perfect Fry PFC570 output is about 60 lb/hr; AutoFry MTI-10 is about 30-60 lb fries/hr, enough for a drive-thru side program — [GoFoodservice](https://www.gofoodservice.com/p/perfect-fry-pfc570-208); [AutoFry](https://ckdr.prod.nomad.site/autofry-mti-10xl-2-75-gallon-ventless-single-basket-countertop-fryer-208-240-v-single-phase-30-amp)
- Sota i1 and Merrychef e2s combine impingement, convection and microwave cooking (accelerated cooking) — [Middleby](https://shop.middleby.com/turbocheftm-sota-i1-countertop-high-speed-oven-ventless-impingement-microwave-assisted-cooking-1-oven-208-240v-1ph-4.html); [Chefstoys](https://chefstoys.com/products/merrychef-e2s-eikon-standard-classic-microwave-convection-oven-208-240v-1000-2200-watts-35680)

### Inferences
- Good fits for the current menu: pizza and flatbread, breakfast sandwiches and burritos (eggs pre-cooked or cooked in the oven), toasted subs and paninis, quesadillas, loaded nachos and pretzels (already offered), baked potatoes (oven or steam), rice or grain bowls (rice cooker plus cold-prep line), chili and soups (kettles/wells), fries, tots and corn dogs (ventless fryer).
- Raw-protein cooking (e.g., raw burgers on a griddle) is the main thing that pushes toward a ventless hood or a real Type I hood. It also adds health-code complexity (raw meat handling, prep sinks).
- Expanding food will likely change the health license category and plan review scope. That is a small fee relative to equipment.

### Gaps
- No sourced throughput or menu data for using a single high-speed oven at drive-thru volumes; this would be an operator-specific question for TurboChef/Merrychef culinary support.

## 3. Oregon / Union County / La Grande rules and process

### Takeaway
There are three approvals: (1) a building/mechanical/electrical/plumbing permit from the City of La Grande Building Division for the TI (2025 OMSC now mandatory); (2) food-service plan review by CHD Environmental Health under Oregon's food rules before construction; and (3) fire-code review of any hood or suppression, usually coordinated through the building and fire officials.

### Cited Findings
**Mechanical code**
- The 2025 Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code is based on the 2024 IMC and IFGC with Oregon amendments (OAR 918-440-0010). It took effect Oct 1, 2025, and is mandatory for new applications from April 1, 2026 — [Oregon BCD adoption notice](https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/laws-rules/Documents/20251001-25omsc-adoption-pr.pdf); [Oregon BCD Mechanical program](https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/mechanical.aspx); [Portland PPD alert](https://www.portland.gov/ppd/commercial-permitting/news/2025/9/1/new-2025-oregon-structural-specialty-code-and-oregon)

**Food sanitation / plan review**
- Under OAR 333-161-0000, plan review is required for new or extensively remodeled food service facilities, and construction must meet OAR 333-150-0000 through 333-156-0330 (as summarized on a Multnomah County form) — [Multnomah County EHS-0025](https://multco.us/file/10998/download)
- Local public health authorities may charge plan review fees under state guidelines (OAR 333-012-0053) — [OAR 333-012-0053](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_333-012-0053)
- Typical Oregon practice: plan review is required for remodels of a licensed facility and for converting existing space. Construction may not begin until plans are approved, and turnaround is about 10-15 business days in metro counties — [Deschutes County packet](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/health_services/page/930/new_restaurant_plan_review_packet.pdf); [Clackamas County](https://www.clackamas.us/publichealth/plan-review)
- Comparison remodel plan-review fees in other Oregon counties: Benton $359 (remodel, first 1.5 hr) / $598 (major); Hood River $427 (limited) / $643 (full, 1/1/26); Washington County $728 minor / $794 major (FY26-27); Coos $580 full-service; Multnomah $890-$1,185 — [Benton](https://health.bentoncountyor.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EHF-0299N-2025-EH-Fee-Sheet.pdf); [Hood River](https://www.hoodrivercounty.gov/vertical/sites/%7B4BB5BFDA-3709-449E-9B16-B62A0A0DD6E4%7D/uploads/1-1-26_-_EH_Fee_Schedule.pdf); [Washington Co.](https://www.washingtoncountyor.gov/environmental-health/documents/restaurant-and-food-service-facilities-fees-2026-27-1/download); [Coos](https://cooscounty-or.specialdistrict.org/files/90465e5c5/public_health.pdf); [Multnomah](https://multco.us/services/restaurant-license)
- Neighboring Umatilla County full-service restaurant plan review: about $439-$457 — [Umatilla County BCC agenda](https://co.umatilla.or.us/fileadmin/user_upload/BCC/Agendas/Item9061825.pdf)

**Union County / CHD**
- The Center for Human Development (CHD), a nonprofit at 2301 Cove Ave, La Grande (541-962-8800), runs Union County's Environmental Health program. It licenses and inspects food service facilities and handles complaints — [CHD Environmental Health](https://www.chdinc.org/environmental-health); [CHD home](https://www.chdinc.org/)
- In 2019 CHD sought a 15% increase in food license fees. The proposed figures were $507/yr for a restaurant with 15 or fewer seats and $725 for 150+ seats; whether this was adopted was not confirmed — [La Grande Observer](https://lagrandeobserver.com/?p=234815)

**City of La Grande building permits**
- La Grande Building Division: (541) 962-1360, M-F 8-5; permit portal at cityoflagrande.org/687/Building-Division (third-party directory, not verified on the city site) — [PermitsAIGE directory](https://permitsaige.com/directory/or/la-grande-or)
- La Grande commercial electrical and plumbing permits show up in the state Oregon BCD Accela system (e.g., 2025 permit numbers 489-25-xxxx) — [Pruuf permit record](https://www.get-pruuf.com/permits/489-25-001212-ELEC)
- Under OSSC Section 105, altering or changing occupancy of a building requires a permit. Mechanical, plumbing and electrical permits may also be needed, and seating or occupancy changes can trigger other requirements (e.g., parking or restrooms) — [Corvallis TI process](https://corvallisoregon.gov/node/1751); [Wilsonville TI guide](https://www.wilsonvilleoregon.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/building/page/96285/bpp_102_commerical_ti_permits_2024_review.pdf)
- Oregon local permit fees are typically based on project valuation using the ICC Building Valuation Data table (Happy Valley example) — [Happy Valley fee schedule](https://www.happyvalleyor.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Bldg-Fees-040819.pdf)

### Inferences
- Turning lobby space into prep/kitchen space is very likely an "extensive remodel" that triggers CHD plan review. It probably also needs a city building permit, plus plumbing (hand sink, 3-compartment sink, prep sink, possibly a grease interceptor) and electrical (208/240V circuits; each ventless unit needs about 20-50A).
- Electrical service capacity may be the binding constraint for an all-electric ventless kitchen; an electrician should check panel capacity early.
- Expect the CHD plan review fee to be in the low hundreds of dollars (similar rural counties: about $360-$650). This is an estimate; the Union County figure was not found.
- Removing lobby seating may reduce restroom and occupancy requirements; adding kitchen area may change ventilation (each recirculating-system appliance counts as 100 sq ft of kitchen for IMC Table 403.3.1.1).

### Gaps
- No current CHD plan review fee, application form or timeline found (site unreachable). Call 541-962-8800.
- No La Grande fee schedule found; also unconfirmed whether the City handles mechanical/plumbing itself or through the state BCD/Union County.
- Oregon State Fire Marshal requirements for ventless units were not researched in detail. Many ventless hoods (e.g., Wells WVU) include or require integrated fire suppression; the local fire department (La Grande Fire) should be consulted.
- Whether Oregon amended IMC 507 (e.g., adopted Washington-style 5 mg/m3 language) remains unverified.

## 4. Cost comparison: full Type I hood vs ventless, and TI costs

### Takeaway
A small Type I hood with suppression, duct and make-up air is commonly quoted at about $12k-$40k+. Low-confidence planning ranges reach $30k-$120k once make-up air, roof work and heating are included. Ventless costs $10-15k per appliance or $18-38k for a ventless hood. TI construction for small food space commonly ranges $80-$250/sq ft, lower if no hood is needed.

### Cited Findings (mostly vendor/SEO sources; low confidence)
- Fire suppression (e.g., Ansul R-102) for a small single-line kitchen: about $3,000-$7,000 installed; another source says $3,500-$6,000 — [The Pricer](https://www.thepricer.org/?p=7324); [Restaurant Warehouse](https://therestaurantwarehouse.com/blogs/restaurant-equipment/commercial-kitchen-hood-installation-cost)
- 2026 hood guide line items: hood about $3,500, ductwork about $3,000, labor about $4,200, permits about $700; exhaust fan plus make-up air $1,800 low / $6,500 average / $18,000 high; whole system $3k low / $12k average / $120k high — [Kurlon cost guide](https://selector.kurlon.com/live/type-1-hood-cost-guide-2026-13746.html); [Rising Sun cost guide](https://new.risingsunartscentre.org/news/commercial-hood-system-cost-guide-2026.html)
- A cafe build-out guide puts hood plus make-up air systems at $30,000-$120,000 and calls them among the costliest items to add later — [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q13726)
- TI / build-out: about $80-$250/sq ft for a cafe (second-gen about $80-$120; raw shell $180-$250); about $50-$150/sq ft when kitchen infrastructure exists; full restaurant $200-$400/sq ft; soft costs about 12-20%; contingency 10-15% — [PulseRevOps](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/q13726); [GrowthFactor](https://www.growthfactor.ai/resources/blog/retail-build-out-costs); [Menuviel](https://blog.menuviel.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-open-a-restaurant-in-maine/); [Rising Sun](https://new.risingsunartscentre.org/news/renovating-a-small-restaurant-cost-price-and-budget-guide-2026.html)
- Converting existing restaurant space costs about 1.5-2 times less than building from scratch (architect quoted) — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/when-second-generation-restaurant-space-makes-sense/829513/)

### Inferences
- Rough scenario for about 200-300 sq ft of lobby converted to prep with sinks, finishes and electrical, no hood: about $16k-$60k construction at $80-$200/sq ft (rural Oregon labor may be lower; unverified), plus $10k-$30k ventless equipment. Adding a Type I hood adds about $15k-$50k+ and needs a roof or wall penetration plus make-up air, which suits gas equipment and raw-protein grilling.
- The break-even: one ventless fryer plus one more high-speed oven (about $25k) costs less than a modest Type I system, and there is no recurring hood-cleaning or suppression-inspection cost. Ventless units do have filter replacement costs.

## 5. Financing, leasing, grants and loans

### Takeaway
Small grants exist intermittently through NEOEDD (Business Oregon EEIP: up to $4k). SBA microloans (up to $50k, average about $13k) and USDA Intermediary Relending loans through regional intermediaries fit equipment purchases. Contact the EOU SBDC in La Grande for current lenders.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 Business Oregon Economic Equity Investment Program small grants administered by NEOEDD for Union and Wallowa County businesses: $48,000 pool; up to $4,000 for existing businesses ($2,000 for new); usable for assets, working capital and marketing — [Elkhorn Media Group](https://elkhornmediagroup.com/2025-eeip-small-grants-available-for-businesses-in-union-and-wallowa-counties)
- NEOEDD provides "consulting, training, financing, referrals" to Northeast Oregon businesses and received a $15M four-year federal award with a planned subgrant program — [Merkley press release](https://www.merkley.senate.gov/es/?p=20649)
- Union County has run small business grant programs (page exists; current status not verified) — [Union County Small Business Grants](https://unioncountyor.gov/small-business-grants/); [2022 Business Assistance Grant info](https://unioncountyor.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Business-Assistance-Grant-2022-Information.pdf)
- SBA Microloans: up to $50,000, average about $13,000, made through nonprofit intermediaries; can fund equipment, fixtures, inventory and working capital, but not real estate or refinancing — [SBA Microloans](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/microloans); [SBA microlender list](https://www.sba.gov/partners/lenders/microloan-program/list-lenders?O=A&page=6)
- GEODC (Greater Eastern Oregon Development Corp., 541-276-6745) offers USDA Intermediary Relending Program loans (example: $10,000, up to 60 months, from 4%, 0.5% fee); service to Union County not confirmed — [GEODC](https://geodc.net/?p=1948); [USDA OR IRP/RMAP chart](https://www.rd.usda.gov/sites/default/files/OR-BP-IRP-RMAP-ProjectChart-10.08.2020.pdf)

### Inferences
- Equipment leasing and financing through dealers (Middleby/TurboChef, WebstaurantStore and similar) is common for $10k-$15k units, but terms were not researched.
- Buying used or refurbished high-speed ovens (seen at $1,200 for a used e2s) is a significant cost lever; check UL listing plates and the health department's acceptance of used equipment (NSF/ANSI).
