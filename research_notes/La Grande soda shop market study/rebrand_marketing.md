Research date: 2026-10-08. About 20 search calls. Several primary pages (Google Help Center, Yelp Support) could not be fetched directly (DNS failure on support.google.com), so their wording below comes from search-result excerpts of those pages and from third-party guides. ## Q1: Rebrand case studies (drink-only identity to food plus drink; small and large brands)

### Takeaway
The big-brand cases that worked (Boston Chicken to Boston Market, Dunkin' Donuts to Dunkin') changed the name to match how the menu and customer behavior had *already* shifted. Gimmicks (IHOb) got short bursts of attention, and a descriptor added to the name that partners didn't take to (Barnie's CoffeeKitchen) was later dropped. I found no well-documented small or independent case of a drink drive-thru adding food and renaming, with published results.

### Cited Findings
- **Boston Chicken to Boston Market (1995):** renamed because the menu had grown past chicken to turkey, ham and meatloaf — [Boston Globe, 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/05/04/lifestyle/boston-market-chicken-downfall/); [The Daily Meal](https://www.thedailymeal.com/1028031/what-boston-market-was-first-called). Trade press at the time said "Market" connotes "fresh, quality, and value" and leaned the chain toward take-out — [Supermarket News](https://www.supermarketnews.com/archive/boston-chicken-changes-name). The restaurant brand changed in 1995; the legal corporate name followed in 1997 — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Boston_Market). (Caution: the chain's later collapse came from finance and expansion problems, not from the name.)
- **Dunkin' Donuts to Dunkin' (January 2019):** described as a move to a "beverage-led, on-the-go brand." It was the first time in about 70 years that "Donuts" was not in the name — [QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/did-dunkins-big-name-change-pay/). In Q1 2019, a 30% jump in espresso drink sales helped same-store sales rise 2.4% and system-wide sales 5.5%. The CEO called it the best sales gains in four years — [1851 Franchise](https://1851franchise.com:443/the-dunkin-recast-into-beverage-business-pays-off-brand-banks-highest-sales-in-four-years-2709865). Technomic estimated 2019 U.S. sales at $9.228B, up 5.0% — [Restaurant Business](https://restaurantbusinessonline.com/top-500-chains-2020/dunkin). Beverages were already about 58% of sales by 2013 — [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15173-coffee-top-draw-for-donut-shop-customers). One analyst said customers already called it "Dunkin'," so the change was not a big deal — [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/discussion/whats-dunkin-without-donuts-in-its-name/). Not every quarter was strong: U.S. same-store sales were flat in the quarter ending Dec 29, 2018 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/02/07/dunkin-shares-drop-after-coffee-chain-focuses-attention-on-espresso.html).
- **IHOP to "IHOb" (2018, temporary rename to sell burgers):** the president said burger sales roughly quadrupled and stayed there; early reports said 4–7x — [FSR Magazine](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/did-ihops-ihob-campaign-drive-sales-results-are/); [AMA](https://www.ama.org/marketing-news/how-a-pancake-house-flipped-to-burgers). MarketWatch, cited by The Takeout, reported only a doubling — [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com:443/ihop-burger-lie-ihob-sales-doubled-1830254502). Same-store sales rose 1.5% for 2018, IHOP's best year since 2015 — [Skift](https://skift.com/?p=6182). An Ansira strategist called it "more of a brand stunt than a brand equity-building campaign" — [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/did-ihops-ihob-campaign-drive-sales-results-are/). These figures are company-reported and the sources conflict.
- **Barnie's Coffee & Tea to "Barnie's CoffeeKitchen" (2011):** added seating, a CIA-trained chef and an on-site kitchen with a food menu. The company later went back to "Barnie's Coffee & Tea" because the CoffeeKitchen name "never really resonated with our retail partners." The Winter Park cafe kept the name — [Scott Joseph Orlando](https://scottjosephorlando.com/?p=3623). This is the closest documented case of a drink brand adding a "Kitchen" descriptor.
- **Small independent examples (no results data published):**
  - You and Me Coffee and Tea became Folktale Coffee and Bakehouse (Corbin, KY) under a new owner, adding breakfast and lunch and renovating the kitchen — [AOL/local news](https://www.aol.com/news/downtown-coffee-shop-rebrands-folktale-153100488.html).
  - Brew Café became Garden Coffee & Cocktails (Worcester, MA), with longer hours — [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/downtown-worcester-coffee-shop-changes-221712064.html).
  - KungBrew Cafe became Fei House (Covington, KY, July 2026) — [LinkNKY](https://linknky.com/culture/2026/07/16/kungbrew-cafe-rebrands-fei-house/).

### Inferences
- Soda Blitz looks like Boston Chicken did: the name names one product and customers don't know about the rest. Dunkin' is the opposite case (it dropped the food word to signal drinks), but it teaches the same lesson: make the name fit what customers actually buy and come in for.
- The IHOb case suggests a playful, temporary, high-attention announcement can make a small-town audience notice new food fast. That would suit Facebook, where Soda Blitz already does well. Pair it with a lasting name, not a gimmick on its own.
- The Barnie's case is a warning that "& Kitchen"-style descriptors can feel awkward or get ignored. Test any descriptor with customers before paying for signage.
- Keeping the familiar root (as Dunkin' and Boston Market did) keeps recognition. "Blitz" could be kept, for example "Blitz Eats & Sodas" (a hypothetical example, not tested).

### Gaps
- I found no published case study with costs and sales results for a *small* drink-only drive-thru (Swig-style) that added food and renamed.
- I found no data on Swig or its competitors renaming or adding substantial food.
- Trade-press pieces on failed QSR renames (e.g., brands that later reverted) were not reviewed beyond Barnie's.

## Q2: Rebrand approaches and keeping Google and Yelp review history and SEO

### Takeaway
Google keeps reviews only for a "minor" name change, where the proper nouns, services and category stay the same. A full rename can count as a new business and lose its reviews. That makes an evolved name or endorsed sub-brand that keeps "Soda Blitz" or "Blitz" the safest choice for review history. Yelp decides case by case, allows 3 categories, and category edits may have to go through Yelp support.

### Cited Findings
- **Google's rule** (from search excerpts of the Google Business Profile help page 3098204): "When a business makes a minor name change or when a business with multiple locations changes its name, we don't remove reviews." "If your business changes its name but doesn't meet the criteria above, it's considered a new business" — [Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3098204?hl=en).
  - The rebranding criteria, quoted secondhand: a profile "may be eligible for rebranding... if you make a minor name change, in which the proper nouns and services described in the business name remain unchanged and the business category remains unchanged" — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/business-name-change-impact-on-reviews.52432/latest). This is secondhand; check the live page.
- Google says reviews can be transferred between profiles if you "inadvertently create and verify a new Business Profile because of a change in a physical location or a change in ownership... contact us." This applies to location or ownership changes, not explicitly to rebrands — [Google Help via SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/knowledge-articles/how-to-keep-your-reviews-after-a-business-name-change/).
- Edit the existing listing; do not create a new one, which risks duplicates and lost reviews — [SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/knowledge-articles/how-to-keep-your-reviews-after-a-business-name-change/).
- Update the website and directory citations first so name, address and phone (NAP) match across the web. A name change can trigger re-verification — [Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/blog/google-my-business-name-change/); [Thee Digital](https://www.theedigital.com/blog/google-business-rebranding).
- **Yelp:**
  - Yelp evaluates "business transitions on a case-by-case basis in order to determine if a new listing is necessary," per a Yelp support reply quoted on a forum — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/yelp-8-mo-listing.62625/post-199347).
  - Yelp supports only 3 categories, and existing listings must be edited in the Yelp UI — [Yext Help](https://help.yext.com/hc/en-us/articles/52850157111707-Yelp-Integration-Overview).
  - A practitioner reports Yelp has locked admin edits to NAP, categories and hours, so changes go through support. This is anecdotal — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/yelp-8-mo-listing.62625/post-199347).
  - Name changes may require verification — [Signpost](https://www.signpost.com/blog/how-to-change-your-name-on-yelp-or-add-a-nickname).

### Inferences
- **Approach ranking for review safety:**
  1. Endorsed or evolved name that keeps "Soda Blitz" (e.g., "Soda Blitz Kitchen", "Soda Blitz Eats & Drinks"). This most likely counts as minor and keeps reviews.
  2. A transitional period of "New Name (formerly Soda Blitz)" in signs and ads. This is usually not allowed in the Google name field, since Google's name guidelines bar extra descriptors (not verified this session).
  3. A full rename. This risks Google treating it as a new business.
- **Fixing muddled categories:**
  - Google: set the primary category to the food-plus-drink identity (e.g., a restaurant-type category) and add secondary categories for soda or beverages. Changing the *primary* category at the same time as the name may affect the "category unchanged" condition. Consider staging the two changes weeks apart.
  - Yelp: pick 3 categories that reflect food first.
- The safest sequence is likely: update the website, Facebook and citations; then rename Google Business Profile in place; then request Yelp changes through support.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Google's help page directly to confirm the current exact wording, including whether "formerly X" is allowed in the name field.
- I could not fetch Yelp's official "substantial business changes" article.
- I found no data on how much ranking or traffic a renamed listing typically loses.

## Q3: Rebrand cost components; Oregon name registration and trademark basics

### Takeaway
A single-location DIY or freelancer rebrand typically runs about $2.5K–$7K for design plus basic collateral. Physical changeovers (signage, menu boards, packaging, uniforms) can add $5K–$50K per location. Agency branding costs $15K–$60K or more. Oregon assumed business name registration is $50 and is renewed every 2 years; it gives no exclusive rights, which only a trademark does.

### Cited Findings
- Basic branding implementation for a small local business (cards, signage, promo items, packaging): about $2,500–$5,000 — [Inkbot Design](https://inkbotdesign.com/?p=241903).
- Small-business rebrand: DIY $2,500–$7,000 vs. professional $20,000–$100,000 — [Oficina Económica de Galicia](https://oficinaeconomicagalicia.xunta.gal/en/faqs-ambitos/how-much-does-it-cost-to-rebrand-a-small-business/). This source is EU and quotes euros.
- Physical locations (exterior signage, interior decor, menus, packaging, uniforms): $5,000–$50,000 per location. Collateral: $3,000–$20,000. Agencies: $15,000–$60,000+ for strategy, name, logo and identity. Online tools: about $200–$2,000 — [Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/financing-your-company-rebranding-strategy). This is a vendor or lender source.
- Real small-restaurant data point: about $4,000 for one exterior wooden sign (Pinnacle Sign Group); the owner skipped window decals to save money — [Springfield Business Journal](https://sbj.net/stories/made-to-order-chapter-seventeen-wrapping-up,66993?).
- Rebrand checklist (menus, table tents, loyalty cards, business cards, window decals, catering brochures, all replaced at once) — [4over4 case study](https://v2.4over4.com/case-study/how-a-restaurant-rebranded-with-custom-printed-menus).
- **Oregon Assumed Business Name:**
  - The fee is $50, and renewal is $50 every 2 years (ORS 648.017 / 648.115). Filing goes through the Secretary of State Corporation Division / Oregon Business Registry — [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/smallbusiness/starting-a-business/how-to-file-a-dba-in-oregon-in-5-steps.html); [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/oregon-dba-registration-and-renewal-how-to-file-an-assumed-business-name).
  - Registration "does not by itself grant exclusive rights to the name"; a trademark does — [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/oregon-dba-guide-how-to-register-an-assumed-business-name).

### Inferences
- Signage is likely the biggest single cost for a drive-thru: pole or building sign, drive-thru menu board and window graphics. Get 2–3 local quotes.
- Cups and packaging can be phased in by using up existing stock, or by switching to a generic cup with a sticker or sleeve during the transition.
- A digital menu board, if already in use, cuts the cost of changing the menu.
- An evolved name that keeps "Soda Blitz" or "Blitz" lowers costs, because some existing assets stay usable.
- **Trademark basics:** before committing to a name, search USPTO trademarks, the Oregon Business Registry, domain availability and social handles. Also check whether "Blitz" conflicts with existing beverage marks. These steps come from general knowledge and were not sourced this session.

### Gaps
- I found no La Grande-area sign shop pricing.
- I found no specific costs for drive-thru menu boards, custom cups or uniforms.
- USPTO filing fees for 2026 (fees changed in January 2025) were not verified this session.
- Oregon's state trademark registration fee was not verified.

- **Elkhorn Media Group:** KCMB, KWRL, KVBL and KHKF, plus a local news website and Facebook pages.
- **Pacific Empire Radio:** KLBM, KUBQ "The Ranch" and KRJT.

Other channels:
- The La Grande Observer, owned by Carpenter Media Group since November 2024.

- EOU: the MAA may offer game-day or banner sponsorships far below the Banner Bank scale. Students can also be reached through campus events.
- Because recent reviews matter (74% want reviews from the last 3 months), plan a review push around the relaunch. Respond to reviews personally, not with templates. Describe the food in replies and Google posts so the new identity shows up in search.
- AI tools have risen as a local recommendation source, per BrightLocal. Consistent descriptions of the food menu across Google, Yelp, the website and Facebook help AI and search tools describe the business correctly.

## Q5: Best practices for a small-town "grand re-opening" after a rebrand

### Takeaway
I found little rigorous or sourced guidance specific to grand re-openings. The best evidence available comes from the case studies above: tie the new name to the menu change (Boston Market), use a surprising or playful reveal to get attention (IHOb), and drive recent reviews (BrightLocal).

### Cited Findings
- IHOb's teaser-then-reveal campaign produced viral attention that the company credited with roughly quadrupling burger sales and a 200-basis-point shift in dinner sales mix in the first three weeks — [FSR Magazine](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/did-ihops-ihob-campaign-drive-sales-results-are/).
- Pairing the IHOb campaign with a value offer ($6.99 deal) helped off-premises business — [FSR Magazine](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/did-ihops-ihob-campaign-drive-sales-results-are/).
- Review recency matters (74% want reviews from the last 3 months) — [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/).
- The Union County Chamber serves all of the county's towns and hosts business events, which suits a ribbon-cutting — [Travel Oregon](https://traveloregon.com/plan-your-trip/oregon-welcome-centers/union-county-chamber-of-commerce/).

### Inferences (not directly sourced; for the report writer to present as recommendations)
1. **Teaser phase** (2–3 weeks): Facebook posts hinting at "something new," building on the "I didn't know you had food" comments.
2. **Reveal day:** a Chamber ribbon-cutting with Observer and Elkhorn news coverage, a radio remote broadcast, and free food samples in the drive-thru line.
3. **Launch week:**
   - A food-plus-drink combo offer.
   - A loyalty sign-up bonus (Square Loyalty).
   - An EOU student-ID deal.
   - A school fundraiser night that gives a share of sales to a local school or team.
4. **Afterward:** ask for reviews that mention the food. Keep "formerly Soda Blitz" in ads and signs for 6–12 months.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative studies of grand re-opening ROI for small restaurants.
- I found no La Grande-specific examples of businesses that rebranded and relaunched.
